Today's blood tests tell you amyloid is there. Sciflux is built to show how the disease is changing.

Every cell releases tiny particles called extracellular vesicles, a snapshot of the cell that made them. Published research shows they change with Alzheimer's. We read thousands of them from a single blood sample and turn them into a fingerprint of disease.

  1. A routine blood drawA small sample from the draw the clinic already does. No spinal tap, no scanner.
  2. Read on our microfluidic chipThousands of particles from one sample, each one read individually.
  3. A fingerprint of diseaseMachine learning turns the readings into one quantitative profile.
  4. Change the clinic can act onEach result is compared with the patient's own baseline, so the neurologist sees what changed since the last visit.
Why it matters

Treatments have arrived. Monitoring hasn't.

The first drugs that slow Alzheimer's reached patients in the last few years. They cost tens of thousands per year, and clinicians still have no objective way to track how each patient responds.
